Arcworld

Rulebook · version 4 · 2–4 players · 60–180 minutes

How to Play

Four corporations land on a planet none of them are willing to share. You expand across sectors, build on them, feed an economy that runs on Arc Cubes, and settle the rest with Operatives and dice. There is no points tally at the end — you win by outlasting everyone or by finishing your Progression track first.

01

Overview & winning

Arcworld is a competitive strategy game for two to four players. Each player runs one corporation, commands a small squad of Operatives across a hex map, claims sectors, constructs Structures, and fights both rival corporations and the planet's native Creatures.

Play runs in rounds. Each round every player takes a full turn of their own, and once everyone has had theirs, the planet takes its turn — Creatures move and attack.

Two ways to win

  • Military victory. Destroy the Headquarters of every opposing corporation.
  • Progression victory. Reach the final black square of your Progression tracker. The game ends immediately and you win.

When a corporation is eliminated

A corporation is eliminated the moment the Control Point of its Headquarters sector is destroyed. Resolve it in this order:

  1. The attacking corporation takes all of the eliminated player's Arc Cubes, Resources and Xeno Eggs.
  2. The Headquarters sector is claimed by the attacker as a normal claim.
  3. Every other sector they controlled is permanently destroyed — nobody may claim it again.
  4. All of their Operatives, Structures and Control Dice return to the box.

03

Setup

  1. Build ArcworldPlace the game board.
  2. Hand out Player BoardsEach player takes a Player Board with their matching tokens and dice.
  3. Prepare the decksShuffle the Corporation, Operative, Encounter and Ability decks separately and place them face-down on the board. Shuffle the Objective deck and place the First Contact card on top of it.
  4. Prepare the event tokensBuild a face-down pile of Tunnel and Creature tokens scaled to the player count: 4 Tunnels and 2 Creatures at four players, 3 Tunnels and 1 Creature at three, and 2 Tunnels with no Creatures at two.
  5. Choose your corporationStarting with the Initiative player and going clockwise, draw 3 Corporation Cards blind, keep 1, discard the rest.
  6. Roll for InitiativeEveryone rolls a die. Highest takes the Initiative Marker; tied players reroll until one wins.
  7. Establish landing sitesIn Initiative order, each player completes all four of these before the next player begins.
    1. Choose any available Sector and place your Headquarters Control Die on its Control Point, set to 10.
    2. Deploy your Troopers on passable tiles adjacent to your Headquarters Control Point.
    3. Apply the Start of the Game effect on your Corporation Card.
    4. Draw 2 Ability Cards as your starting hand.
  8. Place the event tokensStarting with the last player and going counter-clockwise, each player places one token face-down. The Initiative player then places any that remain.
  9. Place neutral Control PointsPut a neutral Control Die on every unclaimed Control Point and set each to 3.
  10. BeginThe Initiative player takes the first turn of Round 1, then play continues clockwise.

04

The round

A round is every player taking one full turn in Initiative order, followed by the Intermission Phase. Your own turn runs through three phases in a fixed order.

  1. Income PhaseReduce the duration tracker on any Contract you have in play by 1, roll your Event Die and resolve it, gain your Arc Cubes and any Resources, then draw 1 Ability Card — in that order.
  2. Action PhaseThe bulk of your turn. See Your Action Phase below.
  3. Cleanup PhaseReassign your available Workers, then discard down to your maximum hand size of 5. Moving into Cleanup is what ends your turn.
Phases are sealed

You may do nothing in the Cleanup Phase except reassign Workers and discard. Likewise the Income Phase allows nothing but its five listed steps.

Arc Cube income

You gain 1 Arc Cube per sector you control, plus 1 more if you have a Worker assigned to Arc Cubes. A sector holding an enemy Operative or Creature is suppressed and pays nothing.

The Event Die

Rolled once during your Income Phase. Four of its six faces are your main source of Resources.

05

Your Action Phase

Two things happen once each per turn, in whichever order you like:

  • Perform 1 active ability with one of your Structures.
  • Move, attack, and/or use an active ability with each of your Operatives.

Activating an Operative

Each Operative may move, attack, and use the active ability on its Operative Card once per Action Phase — but it must do all of it in one go. You cannot move an Operative, switch to another, and come back to it later.

Within a single activation the order is yours: move then attack then use an ability, or attack first, or ability first. You are never forced to do everything — moving only, or using an ability only, is fine.

Between two Operative activations you may freely do anything from the list below.

Free at any point in your Action Phase

  • Play Ability Cards. Cost depends on the tier of the ability you choose: 3 Arc Cubes for a Tier I ability, 6 for Tier II, 9 for Tier III.
  • Construct Structures. As many as you can pay for, each needing a free Worker.
  • Deploy assigned Operatives.
  • Propose a trade. Arc Cubes, Resources and Xeno Eggs may be exchanged freely with any player, in any quantity, and a trade need not be balanced — both players simply have to agree.
Discovery cards

If a card you draw is a Discovery, resolve it immediately for free. It never enters your hand, unlike Orders, Counters and Contracts.

06

Workers

Workers are your labour pool, tracked on your Player Board. Each one sits in an assignment, and each assignment pays differently:

AssignmentEffect
A StructureRequired to construct it. The Worker is locked there for as long as the Structure stands.
Arc CubesGain 1 extra Arc Cube each Income Phase.
OperativesWhen you draw an Operative Card, draw 2 instead and assign or discard either or both.
A ResourceWhenever you gain that Resource type, gain 2 instead.
Xeno EggsWhenever you gain Xeno Eggs, gain 1 extra.

You may move Workers freely between assignments during your Cleanup Phase — except to or from a Structure. A Worker only enters a Structure when you construct it during your Action Phase, and never leaves it: if that Structure is destroyed, its Worker is removed from the game.

07

Combat

All combat uses the same six-sided Combat Die, but you read it differently depending on which side of the attack you are on. One face carries both a Critical and a Block icon, and it is the only face that does something in either direction.

FaceCountAttackingDefending
Hit31 damage—
Block2—1 defense
Critical + Block12 damage1 defense

So an attacking die has four live faces — three Hits and the Critical — and a defending die has three, the two Blocks plus that same shared face. Everything else on the die is blank for that roll.

Attacking an Operative or Creature

  1. The attacker rolls Combat Dice equal to the Attack value on its card. Hits and Criticals total up as the Bio Roll.
  2. The defender rolls Combat Dice equal to its Defense value. Blocks — including the shared Critical face — total up as the Defense Roll.
  3. Bio Roll minus Defense Roll is the Bio Damage dealt.

If the defender is eliminated, the attacker advances their Progression tracker by 1.

Attacking a Control Point

An Operative must be adjacent — within Range 1 — of a Control Point to attack it. The attacker's Hits and Criticals make the Siege Roll. A Control Point always defends with exactly 2 Combat Dice, no matter who owns it or what is stationed there, and the difference is the Siege Damage applied to its Control Die.

When a Control Die reaches 0 the Control Point is destroyed and the attacker claims the sector.

Rerolls

A Reroll lets you pick one die from a roll you just made and roll it again, replacing the old result. A single die can never be rerolled twice, however many Rerolls you hold, and you always choose after seeing the original results.

Xeno Eggs

Spend at any time. Each Egg gives you either +2 Combat Dice before a Bio Roll or restores one of your Operatives to its maximum Energy. Spend as many as you like at once, choosing independently for each.

08

Control Points & sectors

Every sector has one Control Point, marked by a Control Die showing its Sector Strength. Neutral Control Points start at 3; a Headquarters starts at 10.

Claiming

  1. Place your Control Die on the Control Point, set to 3.
  2. Advance your Sectors tracker and your Progression tracker by 1.

Instead of claiming, you may choose to render the sector permanently uninhabitable. Place no die — that sector can never be claimed again by anyone, including Creatures.

Losing

  1. Remove your Control Die.
  2. Reduce your Sectors tracker by 1.
  3. Destroy one of your Structures if you have one — your choice which. Its Worker is removed from the game.
Destroyed by a Creature

If a Creature reduces a Control Point to 0, the loss resolves normally, and the sector is rendered permanently uninhabitable — no one can ever claim it again.

Suppression

A sector you control is suppressed for as long as an enemy Operative or Creature stands in it. Holding the Control Point is not enough — while anything hostile is present:

  • The sector generates no Arc Cubes during your Income Phase.
  • Operatives you deploy into it arrive at half their maximum Energy, rounded up.

Suppression lifts the moment the intruder leaves or dies.

09

Operatives

Assigning

Whenever you draw an Operative Card — by recruiting, from a Barracks, or from any other effect — you must immediately assign it to one of your Operative slots or discard it. Operative Cards are never held in hand.

You may assign to any slot, empty or not. Assigning over an existing Operative discards that card, and if it was deployed its die comes off the board first. The new Operative always arrives assigned but undeployed.

Assigning is permanent

A replaced Operative Card is discarded, not returned to the deck or your hand. Choose carefully before assigning over an Operative you want to keep.

Deploying

At any point in your Action Phase you may deploy an assigned Operative to a tile adjacent to any Control Point you control, your Headquarters included.

No two Operatives may share a tile, and if there is no free space you simply cannot deploy until there is. Deploying is always optional.

Deploying into a suppressed sector — one holding an enemy Operative or Creature — is allowed, but the Operative arrives at half its maximum Energy, rounded up.

The Trooper

Your Troopers fill your Operative slots at the start of the game and can never be permanently removed. An eliminated Trooper simply returns to its card, assigned and undeployed, ready to deploy again.

Assigning another Operative to a Trooper's slot benches the Trooper — it can no longer be deployed, and comes off the board if it was out. If that replacement Operative is later eliminated and its card removed, the slot reverts to the Trooper.

Elimination

An Operative that loses all its Energy is removed from the board and its card discarded. If a rival corporation eliminated it, that player advances their Progression tracker by 1.

10

Creatures

Up to four Creatures may be on the board at once, each marked with a numbered Creature Die from 1 to 4. That die is only an identifier for movement order — it is never rolled. If a fifth Creature would deploy while four are out, the deployment simply fails; nothing already on the board is removed.

The Intermission Phase

Creatures act one at a time, in Creature Die order from lowest to highest. For each Creature:

  1. Roll the Intermission DieThe result sets which way this Creature moves, using the arrows on its Encounter Card.
  2. Attack if it canIf one or more enemies are within its attack range, it attacks them and does not move. A Creature on the Volcano tile can never attack from there, so it always goes straight to step 3.
  3. Otherwise, move and check againIt moves its full Movement in the direction rolled. If an enemy is now within range, it attacks as in step 2.
  • Movement cannot be split. A Creature that cannot complete its full move goes as far as it can and stops.
  • A Creature that leaves the edge of the map is redeployed on the Volcano tile and its movement ends.

A Creature's target is always an enemy Operative, or an enemy Control Point if that Creature can perform a Siege attack.

Blocked tiles

Creatures never end movement on a tile holding another Creature, an Operative, a Structure or a Control Point. They may move through friendly Creatures and neutral Control Points if they have the Movement to spare — which takes at least 2, one to enter and one to leave.

Killing and subjugating Creatures

Eliminating a Creature with an Operative advances your Progression tracker by 1 and earns you 1 Xeno Egg.

Subjugation

When one of your Operatives would eliminate a Creature, you may subjugate it instead. You forgo the Progression advance. Place the Encounter Card on your Subjugated Creature slot and restore its Health fully — it now counts as an Operative under your control.

11

Tiers & Progression

Every corporation starts at Tier 1 and climbs to 3 along the Progression tracker on its Player Board. Tier 1 is blue, Tier 2 purple, Tier 3 red — the same colors used on Ability Cards.

Advancing the tracker

Move forward 1 square each time you destroy a Control Point, whether neutral or enemy-held; eliminate an enemy Operative or a Creature; or gain Progression from any other effect.

Landing on a reward square immediately grants either 1 Ability Card or 3 Arc Cubes, depending on the icon. Landing on the Tier 2 or Tier 3 square advances you immediately — there is no cost, no action, and no way to delay it. Reaching the final black square ends the game and wins it.

What advancing changes

  • Operative Energy. Your Operatives keep their current Energy, but their maximum rises, so anything that restores Energy can now fill them higher.
  • Ability Cards. You unlock that tier's abilities and keep access to every lower tier.
  • Creatures. All Creatures use the stats of the highest Tier any player has reached. They keep their current Health but their maximum rises with it.

15

The cards

Corporation Cards give you a starting supply and one or more permanent passive abilities. You keep one for the whole game.

Operative Cards carry the figures you put on the board — Movement, Energy, Defense, Range, Attack values, and passive or active abilities.

Encounter Cards are the planet's Creatures: movement direction arrows for each Intermission Die face, Movement, Health, Defense, Range and attack values.

Ability Cards hold three abilities each — one per Tier. You may play any single ability at or below your current Tier, paying 3, 6 or 9 Arc Cubes for Tier I, II or III. An ability is an Order (your turn), a Counter (someone else's turn, on a condition), a Contract (stays in play three rounds) or a Discovery (resolves free the moment it is drawn).

Objective Cards are revealed one at a time and give everyone a shared side goal to race for.

How a card is read

Each card stacks its three tiers top to bottom, and each ability carries its own type. The rule of the deck is that you pick one — the other two are spent with it.

Tiers

Tier I at the top, Tier II in the middle, Tier III at the bottom, colored here as they are in print. Tier is a position on the card, not a cost — a card is worth as much as the one ability you take from it.

Types

Orders resolve on your own turn. Counters wait for someone else's turn and a matching condition. Contracts stay on your board and tick down three rounds before they are discarded — the numbered boxes on the card are that countdown.

Hand

One card drawn per turn, five held. Go over during your turn and you discard back down to five in your cleanup phase, so a card kept for its Tier III is a card not spent on tempo.

Objective deck · 5 cards · one face-up at a time

Objectives

A shared side goal that everyone races at once. You do not choose to take one on — it sits face-up on the table, and the first player to do the thing places a tracker token on it. When the card fills up, the player with the most tokens on it takes first place.

5Objectives
1Face-up at a time
3Tokens to fill a card
3Places paid

How they work

First Contact always starts on top; the rest are shuffled beneath it. Only the top card is ever active.

Placing tokens

Whenever you meet a criterion on the face-up objective, put one of your tracker tokens on it. On First Contact each of the four criteria is a separate slot with its own immediate reward, claimable once. On every other card the tokens all go to the same goal, and nothing is paid out until the card is full.

Resolving

The moment the last token lands, count tokens per player. Most tokens takes 1st, next takes 2nd, then 3rd. Players with no tokens get nothing, and any place nobody reaches simply goes unpaid.

Ties drop a place

Players on equal tokens all slide down one place and take that reward instead. Two players tied at the top means nobody takes 1st — both take the 2nd-place reward.

The deck

Every card past First Contact pays the same shape: first place adds Progression on top of the themed reward, second place gets the themed reward alone, and third gets a single card for showing up.

Reading a card

Laid out like an Operative Card, with two differences: the ten-box strip is Health rather than Energy, and Creatures have no active abilities — everything they do is passive, resolved for them during the Intermission Phase.

A Creature only threatens a Control Point if it can perform a Siege attack. The ones that cannot say so in plain text on the card, and they can still make life miserable by suppressing the sector they stand in.
