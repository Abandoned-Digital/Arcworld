Arcworld

Rulebook · version 4 · 2–4 players · 60–180 minutes

How to Play

Four corporations land on a planet none of them are willing to share. You expand across Sectors, build on them, feed an economy that runs on Arc Cubes, and settle the rest with Operatives and dice. There is no points tally at the end — you win by outlasting everyone or by finishing your Progression track first.

01

Overview & winning

Arcworld is a competitive strategy game for two to four players. Each player runs one corporation, commands a small squad of Operatives across a hex map, claims Outposts, constructs Structures, and fights both rival corporations and the planet's native Creatures.

Play runs in rounds. Each round every player takes a full turn of their own, and once everyone has had theirs, the planet takes its turn — Creatures move and attack.

Two ways to win

  • Military victory. Destroy the Headquarters of every opposing corporation.
  • Progression victory. Reach the final black square of your Progression tracker. The game ends immediately and you win.

When a corporation is eliminated

A corporation is eliminated the moment its Headquarters is destroyed. Resolve it in this order:

  1. The attacking corporation takes all of the eliminated player's Arc Cubes, Resources and Xeno Eggs.
  2. The Headquarters Sector is claimed by the attacker as a normal claim.
  3. Every Outpost they held is permanently destroyed — nobody may claim it again.
  4. All of their Operatives, Structures and dice return to the box.

03

Setup

  1. Build ArcworldPlace the game board. Shuffle the 20 Sector tiles and draw 16 of them blind; the other four go back in the box unseen. With three players, remove 4 of the 16 at random and replace them with 4 empty Sector tiles; with two players, remove 8 at random and replace them with 8. Shuffle the 16 into a face-down stack beside the board — they fill the 16 empty Sector slots as those are discovered.
  2. Hand out Player BoardsEach player takes a Player Board with their matching tokens and dice.
  3. Prepare the decksShuffle the Corporation, Operative, Encounter and Ability decks separately and place them face-down on the board. Place the Incentives Objective card face-up beside the board.
  4. Choose your corporationStarting with the Initiative player and going clockwise, draw 3 Corporation Cards blind, keep 1, discard the rest.
  5. Roll for InitiativeEveryone rolls a die. Highest takes the Initiative Marker; tied players reroll until one wins.
  6. Establish landing sitesIn Initiative order, each player completes all four of these before the next player begins.
    1. Choose any landing site no one has taken — one of the six four-tile Sectors on the rim of the board — and place your Headquarters die on its landing site, set to 10.
    2. Deploy your Troopers on passable tiles adjacent to your Headquarters.
    3. Apply the Start of the Game effect on your Corporation Card.
    4. Draw 2 Ability Cards as your starting hand.
  7. BeginThe Initiative player takes the first turn of Round 1, then play continues clockwise.

04

The round

Play runs clockwise around the table, one full turn each. The Initiative Marker never changes hands — it stays with whoever won it at setup and marks where a round ends: once play comes back around to that player, the Intermission Phase resolves before their next turn begins. Your own turn runs through three phases in a fixed order.

  1. Income PhaseReduce the duration tracker on any Contract you have in play by 1, roll your Event Die and resolve it, gain your Arc Cubes and any Resources, then draw 1 Ability Card — in that order.
  2. Action PhaseThe bulk of your turn. See Your Action Phase below.
  3. Cleanup PhaseReassign your available Workers, then discard down to your maximum hand size of 5. Moving into Cleanup is what ends your turn. A Worker in a Structure is never available, and one in your Contract slot is unavailable while a Contract is in play.
Phases are sealed

You may do nothing in the Cleanup Phase except reassign Workers and discard. Likewise the Income Phase allows nothing but its five listed steps.

Arc Cube income

You gain 1 Arc Cube per Outpost you hold, or 2 for your Headquarters, plus 1 more if you have a Worker assigned to Arc Cubes. An Outpost or Headquarters in a Sector holding an enemy Operative or Creature is Suppressed and pays nothing.

Rolled once during your Income Phase. Four of its six faces are your main source of Resources.

Once play returns to the Initiative player, the planet acts before their turn begins: each Creature attacks or moves in turn — see Creatures.

05

Your Action Phase

Two things happen once each per turn, in whichever order you like:

  • Perform 1 active ability with one of your Structures.
  • Move, attack, and/or use an active ability with each of your Operatives.

Activating an Operative

Each Operative may move, attack, and use the active ability on its Operative Card once per Action Phase — but it must do all of it in one go. You cannot move an Operative, switch to another, and come back to it later.

Within a single activation the order is yours: move then attack then use an ability, or attack first, or ability first. You are never forced to do everything — moving only, or using an ability only, is fine.

Between two Operative activations you may freely do anything from the list below.

Free at any point in your Action Phase

  • Play Ability Cards. Cost depends on the Tier of the ability you choose: 3 Arc Cubes for a Tier I ability, 6 for Tier II, 9 for Tier III.
  • Recruit an Operative. Spend 3 Arc Cubes to draw the top card of the Operative deck, then assign or discard it at once.
  • Construct Structures. As many as you can pay for, each needing a free Worker.
  • Deploy assigned Operatives.
  • Propose a trade. Arc Cubes, Resources and Xeno Eggs may be exchanged freely with any player, in any quantity, and a trade need not be balanced — both players simply have to agree.
Discovery Cards

If a card you draw is a Discovery, resolve it immediately for free and discard it. It never enters your hand and never counts against your hand limit. This applies to every draw, including the one in your Income Phase and any a card hands you mid-turn.

06

Combat

All combat uses the same six-sided Combat Die, but you read it differently depending on which side of the attack you are on. One face carries both a Critical and a Block icon, and it is the only face that does something in either direction.

FaceCountAttackingDefending
Hit31 damage—
Block2—1 defense
Critical + Block12 damage1 defense

So an attacking die has four live faces — three Hits and the Critical — and a defending die has three, the two Blocks plus that same shared face. Everything else on the die is blank for that roll.

Attacking an Operative or Creature

The target must be within the attacker's Range — the second number along the bottom of its card.

  1. The attacker rolls Combat Dice equal to the Attack value on its card. Hits and Criticals total up as the Bio Roll.
  2. The defender rolls Combat Dice equal to its Defense value. Blocks — including the shared Critical face — total up as the Defense Roll.
  3. Bio Roll minus Defense Roll is the Bio Damage dealt.

If the defender is eliminated, the attacker advances their Progression tracker by 1.

Attacking Settlements, Outposts and Headquarters

An Operative must be adjacent — within Range 1 — of a Settlement, Outpost or Headquarters to attack it. The attacker's Hits and Criticals make the Siege Roll. A Settlement defends with exactly 1 Combat Die and an enemy-held Outpost or Headquarters with exactly 2 Combat Dice.

When a die reaches 0 the Settlement or Outpost is destroyed, and the attacker chooses to claim it or render it permanently uninhabitable.

TerrainEffect on Operatives

A successful Attack

An Attack is successful when it deals 1 or more Damage after the defender's roll is subtracted. Card text names which kind it wants: “on a successful Bio Attack” and “on a successful Siege Attack” each trigger off that one kind only, while “on a successful Attack” triggers off either.

Rerolls

A Reroll lets you pick one die from a roll you just made and roll it again, replacing the old result. A single die can never be rerolled twice, however many Rerolls you hold, and you always choose after seeing the original results.

Xeno Eggs

Spend at any time. Each Egg gives you either +2 Combat Dice before a Bio Roll or restores one of your Operatives to its maximum Energy. Spend as many as you like at once, choosing independently for each.

07

Settlements, Outposts & Sectors

Every Sector tile carries one or two Settlements — the empty Sector tiles used at two and three players carry none. A Settlement is unclaimed ground: a Lava Field marked with a die showing the strength it starts at. A corporation that claims one makes it an Outpost of its own. Each of the six four-tile landing-site Sectors on the rim carries a landing site instead, where a corporation places its Headquarters. Settlements and Outposts are D6s and can be raised as far as 6; a Headquarters is a D12, starts at 10 and can be raised as far as 12. What you count, and what pays, is your Outposts and Headquarters; the Sector is what many effects target. You control a Sector only while you hold every Settlement in it as an Outpost — holding one of two is not control.

Discovering Sectors

The board starts with 16 empty Sector slots, and the 20 Sector tiles wait in a face-down stack beside it. The moment an Operative moves onto a tile adjacent to an empty slot, that Sector is discovered: that Operative's owner draws the top tile blind and places it in the slot, rotated however they like. Then put a die on each Settlement on the tile, set to the strength printed there, and deploy a Creature on each Encounter icon if a Creature slot is free. An Operative can never move into an empty slot.

Claiming

  1. Place your die on the Settlement or Outpost.
  2. Put a tracker token on the Claim Outposts row of the Incentives Objective and take its Fortify 3. Spread it across your Outposts and Headquarters as you like, the new Outpost included — whatever it receives is its starting strength.
  3. Advance your HQ + Outposts tracker and your Progression tracker by 1.

Give the new Outpost none of that Fortify and it is rendered permanently uninhabitable instead: take your die back, and it can never be claimed again by anyone, including Creatures. The reward becomes Fortify 6, spread across your other Outposts and Headquarters, and your HQ + Outposts tracker does not advance.

A card that claims at a set value, such as Annex or Conquest, sets the new Outpost's strength itself; you still take the Incentives row and its Fortify 3.

Losing

  1. Remove your die.
  2. Reduce your HQ + Outposts tracker by 1.
  3. Destroy one of your Structures if you have one — your choice which. Its Worker is removed from the game.
Destroyed by a Creature

If a Creature reduces an Outpost to 0, the loss resolves normally and the Outpost is rendered permanently uninhabitable — no one can ever claim it again. A Headquarters is no exception: that corporation is eliminated exactly as if a rival had done it, except that nothing changes hands. Its Arc Cubes, Resources and Xeno Eggs are gone, and its Headquarters Sector leaves play with the others.

Suppression

An Outpost or Headquarters you hold is Suppressed for as long as an enemy Operative or Creature stands in its Sector. It makes no difference how many Settlements the Sector has or who holds the others — every Outpost you hold there is Suppressed. Holding it is not enough — while anything hostile is present:

  • It generates no Arc Cubes during your Income Phase.
  • It cannot be Fortified, from any source.
  • Operatives you deploy next to it arrive at half their maximum Energy, rounded down.

Suppression lifts the moment the intruder leaves or dies.

Every tile costs 1 Movement to enter unless the table says otherwise.

Creatures are native to all of it. No terrain is impassable to them and every tile costs a Creature 1 Movement, Lava Fields and Mountains included. The Volcano is where they deploy, and where any Creature pushed off the edge of the map reappears; they can never choose to move back onto it.

The Hostile Movement Penalty

Moving an Operative within a Sector controlled by an enemy corporation costs 1 extra Movement per tile. A Sector that no one controls — neutral, or split between corporations — is free of it. So in hostile territory an ordinary tile costs 2 and a Mountain costs 3.

Moving past other figures

Two things never share a tile. Nothing may end its move on a tile already holding an Operative, a Creature, a Settlement or an Outpost — not even your own, and not even your own Headquarters. You may move through anything that is not hostile to you: your own Operatives, your own subjugated Creature, your own Outposts and Headquarters, and for a wild Creature, other Creatures. Passing through costs the Movement to enter the tile and the Movement to leave it, so at least 2. Everything else blocks you outright — including a Settlement, which stops you exactly as an enemy Outpost does.

09

Workers

Workers are your labor pool, tracked on your Player Board. You begin the game with 1 available Worker. Each one sits in an assignment, and each assignment pays differently:

AssignmentEffect
A StructureRequired to construct it. The Worker is locked there for as long as the Structure stands.
Arc CubesGain 1 extra Arc Cube each Income Phase.
OperativesWhen you draw an Operative Card, draw 2 instead and assign or discard either or both.
A ResourceWhenever you gain that Resource type, gain 2 instead.
Xeno EggsWhenever you gain Xeno Eggs, gain 1 extra.
ContractsEvery Contract you play lasts 1 round longer.

You may move Workers freely between assignments during your Cleanup Phase — except to or from a Structure. A Worker only enters a Structure when you construct it during your Action Phase, and never leaves it: if that Structure is destroyed, its Worker is removed from the game. If a Structure is replaced rather than destroyed, its Worker moves across to the replacement.

A Worker on your Contract slot is stuck in the same way, but only while a Contract is running: you cannot take it back until the slot is empty.

10

Structures

Structures are built onto your Player Board, not placed on the map. Construct one during your Action Phase by paying its Resource cost and assigning one free Worker. The number beside it is the Fortify value you gain the moment it is built.

Fortify

Fortify X means add X strength in total, divided between your Outposts and Headquarters however you like — all of it on one die, or spread around. No Outpost may pass its maximum of 6, or a Headquarters its 12; anything over that is lost.

A Suppressed Outpost or Headquarters cannot be Fortified at all. While an enemy stands in its Sector you cannot shore it up — you have to remove them first.

11

Operatives

You have three Operative slots on your Player Board: two for Operatives you recruit, and a third reserved solely for a Creature you subjugate.

Assigning

Whenever you draw an Operative Card — by recruiting, from a Barracks, or from any other effect — you must immediately assign it to one of your Operative slots or discard it. Operative Cards are never held in hand.

You may assign to any slot, empty or not. Assigning over an existing Operative discards that card, and if it was deployed its die comes off the board first. The new Operative always arrives assigned but undeployed.

Assigning is permanent

A replaced Operative Card is discarded, not returned to the deck or your hand. Choose carefully before assigning over an Operative you want to keep.

Deploying

At any point in your Action Phase you may deploy an assigned Operative to a tile adjacent to any Outpost you hold, or to your Headquarters.

No two Operatives may share a tile, and if there is no free space you simply cannot deploy until there is. Deploying is always optional.

Deploying next to a Suppressed Outpost or Headquarters — one whose Sector holds an enemy Operative or Creature — is allowed, but the Operative arrives at half its maximum Energy, rounded down.

The Trooper

Your Troopers are printed on your Player Board rather than dealt from the Operative deck, and they fill your two Operative slots at the start of the game. A Trooper can never be permanently removed: an eliminated Trooper simply returns to its slot, assigned and undeployed, ready to deploy again.

Assigning another Operative to a Trooper's slot benches the Trooper — it can no longer be deployed, and comes off the board if it was out. If that replacement Operative is later eliminated and its card removed, the slot reverts to the Trooper.

Elimination

An Operative that loses all its Energy is removed from the board and its card discarded. If a rival corporation eliminated it, that player advances their Progression tracker by 1.

12

Creatures

Up to four Creatures may be on the board at once, each marked with a numbered Creature Die from 1 to 4. That die is only an identifier for movement order — it is never rolled. If a fifth Creature would deploy while four are out, the deployment simply fails; nothing already on the board is removed.

The Intermission Phase

Creatures act one at a time, in Creature Die order from lowest to highest, each resolved in full before the next begins. For each Creature:

  1. Roll for directionThe result picks one of the six numbered directions marked on the Volcano tile — that is the way this Creature moves.
  2. Act on where it stands
    1. On the Volcano tile. It moves its full Movement off the Volcano in the direction rolled, then checks: if an enemy is within Range, it attacks.
    2. Elsewhere, already within Range of an enemy. It attacks and does not move.
    3. Elsewhere, with nothing in Range. It moves its full Movement in the direction rolled, then checks again. If an enemy is now within Range, it attacks.

A Creature that attacks hits every enemy within Range once. Resolve Bio Attacks first, taking Operatives in order of their Operative Die from lowest to highest; where two corporations share a number, go in Initiative order clockwise. A Creature that can Siege then attacks the Settlement, Outpost or Headquarters it is adjacent to, if there is one.

  • Movement cannot be split. A Creature that cannot complete its full move goes as far as it can and stops.
  • A Creature that leaves the edge of the map is redeployed on the Volcano tile and its movement ends.

A Creature's targets are enemy Operatives within its Range, plus the enemy Outpost or Headquarters on an adjacent tile if that Creature can perform a Siege Attack. Since attacking one needs adjacency — the same rule players follow — a Creature can never reach two at once.

Killing and subjugating Creatures

Eliminating a Creature with an Operative advances your Progression tracker by 1 and puts a token on the Eliminate Creatures row of Incentives, which pays your 1 Xeno Egg. The kill itself pays no other Xeno Egg.

Subjugation

When one of your Operatives would eliminate a Creature, you may subjugate it instead. You forgo both the Progression advance and the Xeno Egg. Place the Encounter Card on your Subjugated Creature slot and restore its Health fully — it now counts as an Operative under your control.

13

Tiers & Progression

Every corporation starts at Tier 1 and climbs to 3 along the Progression tracker on its Player Board. Tier 1 is blue, Tier 2 purple, Tier 3 red — the same colors used on Ability Cards.

Advancing the tracker

Move forward 1 square each time you destroy a Settlement or an enemy Outpost or Headquarters; eliminate an enemy Operative or a Creature; or gain Progression from any other effect.

Landing on a reward square immediately grants either 1 Ability Card or 3 Arc Cubes, depending on the icon. Landing on the Tier 2 or Tier 3 square advances you immediately — there is no cost, no action, and no way to delay it. Reaching the final black square ends the game and wins it.

What advancing changes

Advancing unlocks that Tier's abilities on your Ability Cards, and you keep every Tier beneath it. Each card carries three abilities, one per Tier, and you may play any of them at or below your current Tier for its cost:

TierCost to play an ability
Tier I3 Arc Cubes
Tier II6 Arc Cubes
Tier III9 Arc Cubes

A Tier III corporation therefore has the whole deck open to it — reaching for a card's strongest ability when the moment is worth 9 Arc Cubes, and taking its Tier I ability for 3 when it is not.

14

Objectives

One Objective card — Incentives — sits face-up in play from setup to the end of the game.

It carries four targets, each on its own row with its own reward and its own five token boxes: claim Outposts for Fortify 3 (Fortify 6 if you render the Settlement or Outpost uninhabitable instead), construct Structures for 3 Arc Cubes, eliminate Creatures for 1 Xeno Egg, eliminate Operatives for 1 Worker. Whenever you do one of these things, put one of your tracker tokens in the next free box on that row and take the reward at once.

The rows are independent — a token on one counts for nothing on the others. When the fifth token lands on a row, count that row's tokens per player. Whoever holds the most gains 1 Progression, and every player level on the most takes first place together and each gains 1 Progression; there is no second place. Then take all five tokens off that row and it is open again, as often as the game lasts.

16

The cards

Corporation Cards give you a starting supply and one or more permanent passive abilities. You keep one for the whole game.

Operative Cards carry the figures you put on the board — Movement, Range, Attack and Defense along the bottom, an Energy track, and passive or active abilities. Every value is fixed for the whole game.

Encounter Cards are the planet's Creatures: Movement, Health, Defense, Range and Attack values, plus their passive abilities.

Ability Cards hold three abilities each — one per Tier. You may play any single ability at or below your current Tier, paying 3, 6 or 9 Arc Cubes for Tier I, II or III. An ability is an Order (your turn), a Counter (someone else's turn, on a condition) or a Contract (stays in play two rounds).

One Contract at a time

Only one Contract may sit in front of you. Discard it whenever you like, and playing a new Contract replaces it. A Worker assigned to your Contract slot adds a round to every Contract you play.

Discovery Cards are shuffled through the same deck and drawn the same way, but they are not abilities and never enter your hand. A Discovery resolves the moment you draw it, costs nothing, and is discarded. Most are a windfall; a few are not.

The Objective card sits out all game and gives everyone four shared targets to race for, each paying out on its own — see Objectives.

A card beats this manual

Where a card's text contradicts a rule written here, the card wins. Cards are written as deliberate exceptions: Sentinel Sieges from Range 2 where the rule says Range 1, Cinderworks walks onto Lava Fields where the rule says impassable. If the card says it, it happens.

How a card is read

Each card stacks its three tiers top to bottom, and each ability carries its own type. The rule of the deck is that you pick one — the other two are spent with it.

Tiers

Tier I at the top, Tier II in the middle, Tier III at the bottom, colored here as they are in print. Tier is a position on the card, not a cost — a card is worth as much as the one ability you take from it.

Types

Orders resolve on your own turn. Counters wait for someone else's turn and a matching condition. Contracts stay on your board and tick down two rounds before they are discarded — the numbered boxes on the card are that countdown.

Hand

One card drawn per turn, five held. Go over during your turn and you discard back down to five in your cleanup phase, so a card kept for its Tier III is a card not spent on tempo.

Objective card · one, out all game

Objectives

A single card sits on the table from setup to the last turn, carrying four targets that everyone races at once. Do one of the four and you take that row's reward on the spot. Fill a row and it pays Progression, clears, and opens again.

How they work

The four rows are four separate races. Nothing you do on one has any bearing on another.

Claim and get paid

Meet one of the four targets and put a tracker token in the next free box on that row, then take that row's reward at once. Every claim pays, whether or not the row ever fills. A claim's Fortify 3 also sets the new Outpost's strength: give it none, and it is rendered uninhabitable and pays Fortify 6 instead.

Four separate races

Each row has its own reward and its own five boxes. A token on one counts for nothing on the others — you can pour everything into one target or spread across all four.

Filling a row

When the fifth token lands, count that row's tokens per player. The most gains 1 Progression, and everyone level on the most takes first place together, each gaining 1 Progression. Then lift all five tokens off and the row is open again.

The card

The printed panel at 1:1, laid out here as a card for easy reference. Four targets, five boxes each.

Card text as printed. Every filled row pays 1 Progression to whoever holds the most tokens in it, which also nudges you along the Tier track — the targets are worth chasing for that alone.

Releases

3.20

The board

  • The board is 166 printed tiles around the volcano plus 16 empty Sector slots. 20 Sector tiles are drawn blind and placed, in any rotation, the moment an Operative moves onto a tile next to an empty slot.
  • Settlement, Outpost and Headquarters replace “Control Point”: an unclaimed one is a Settlement, a claimed one is its owner's Outpost, and your landing-site one is your Headquarters. “Sector Strength” becomes plain strength; Settlements and Outposts are D6s, a Headquarters a D12.
  • Settlements carry a printed strength from 1 to 6, and a Settlement die starts there instead of at 3. A Sector tile holds one or two.
  • Fortify X is the one form: add X strength, divided between your Outposts and Headquarters. “Fortify your Sectors by X” is gone from the cards.
  • The player board tracks HQ + Outposts, not Sectors, starting at 2 from the Headquarters, and each Outpost you hold pays 1 Arc Cube in the Income Phase — 2 for your Headquarters.
  • Eight empty Sector tiles join the set: with three players four of the 16 drawn tiles are swapped for empty ones, with two players eight. They carry no Settlement, only a share of the Encounters and Tunnels.
  • You control a Sector only while you hold every Settlement in it as an Outpost. Suppression is per Outpost: one enemy in the Sector Suppresses each Outpost you hold there.
  • Headquarters start on one of six four-tile landing-site Sectors on the rim of the board, rather than on a drawn Sector tile.
  • Tunnels and Encounters are printed on the board and the Sector tiles. The face-down Tunnel and Creature tokens, and their player-count scaling, are gone; a Creature starts on every Encounter icon.
  • Scientists restore 1 Energy to friendly Operatives each Income Phase, in place of the Bio Damage after a successful Bio Attack. Engineers read Fortify 2.
  • Fault Line, Orbital Relay and Red Sector Inc. speak of the Settlement or Outpost rendered uninhabitable, not the Sector.
  • Permafrost is removed from the terrain table. The Sector Atlas shows the printed board, all 20 Sector tiles and the tokens on them.

3.19

Combat

  • A neutral Control Point defends with 1 Combat Die rather than 2. Enemy-held Control Points still defend with 2.

3.18

Discoveries

  • Discoveries drop to 13: Recovered Intel, Unstable Ruins and Corrupted Archive are retired.
  • Orbital Relay deals 2 Siege Damage rather than 3, and Fault Line and Orbital Relay both render the Sector uninhabitable if they destroy the Control Point. Corrosive Bloom costs 1 Resource rather than 2.

3.17

Incentives

  • A claimed Control Die no longer starts at 3. Its Sector Strength comes out of the Fortify 3 from the Claim Control Points row on Incentives, spread across your Control Points as you like.
  • Give the new die none of it and the Sector is rendered uninhabitable, paying Fortify 6 to your other Sectors instead.
  • The Xeno Egg for eliminating a Creature is the one from the Eliminate Creatures row on Incentives — there is no second egg for the kill.

3.16

Ability Cards

  • Six cards added, completing the Structure, Heist and Tariff sets. Foundry, Refinery, Science Academy, Xeno Lab, Base Turret and Shield Generator join at Tier I; Biofuel Tariff, Silica Tariff and Cryofluid Heist at Tier II.
  • Dissolution drops from Tier III to Tier II. Conglomerate takes its old place: for the rest of the game you also gain the passive abilities of every other Corporation Card in play.
  • Also new: Overdrive and Pillage at Tier II; Bastion, Overclock, Expropriation, Stasis Field, Warpath and Hardened Core at Tier III.
  • Overclock lets you use 2 Active Structure abilities each turn for the rest of the game. Warpath lets each of your Operatives attack up to 3 times this turn.
  • The new Tariffs sit on cards without a Structure: Silica Tariff trades places with Signal Jam, and Biofuel Tariff with Iron Defense.
  • Maximum Yield refills Arc Cubes, Resources and Xeno Eggs together, rather than one of the three. Global Tariffs also pays 3 Arc Cubes each time it triggers. Cataclysm destroys 3 Structures of your choice rather than all of them. Clearance hits every other corporation, and Workers on a Contract are safe.
  • Ghost Protocol stops enemy effects and abilities targeting your Operatives; it does not stop Attacks. Xeno Eggs from Egg Heist beyond your storage limit are lost. Extinction Event and Defector earn no Progression.
  • The deck grows to 42 cards and 126 abilities, 22 of them Contracts.
  • Discoveries drop to 16: Dormant Conduit and Broken Seal are retired. Salvaged Cache gives 2 Resources, Lone Survivor no longer sets the Operative's Energy, and Something Stirs deploys its Creature on the Volcano tile rather than beside your Operatives.

3.15

Plural names

  • Every Operative is named in the plural — each card is a squad, not a lone figure: Hunters, Engineers, Shock Troopers and the rest of the 36.
  • Three irregular plurals: Foreman becomes Foremen, Marksman becomes Marksmen, Mercenary becomes Mercenaries.
  • Creatures that hunt in numbers are named in the plural too — Sylth Grunts, Gorepods, Brood Leeches and eleven more. The four lone giants keep their singular names: Sylth Titan, Overseer, Overmind and Overlord.

3.14

Objectives

  • The Objective deck becomes a single permanent card, Incentives, face-up from setup to the end of the game. First Contact, Entrenchment and Total Dominion are retired, and objectives no longer run out.
  • It carries four targets, each on its own row with its own reward and its own five token boxes: Control Points for Fortify 3, Structures for 3 Arc Cubes, Creatures for 1 Xeno Egg, Operatives for 1 Worker.
  • Meeting a target places a token on that row and pays its reward at once, whether or not the row ever fills.
  • Filling a row's five boxes pays 1 Progression to whoever holds the most tokens in that row, ties all paying. The row then clears and reopens, as often as the game lasts.
  • The rows are independent: a token on one counts for nothing on the others.

3.13

Objectives

  • The Objective deck drops to three cards and is stacked, not shuffled: First Contact, then Entrenchment, then Total Dominion.
  • Place-based payouts are gone. Every card carries the same four criteria — claim a Control Point, construct a Structure, eliminate a Creature, eliminate an Operative — each on its own row, each paying the moment you claim it.
  • Rows carry one token box on First Contact, two on Entrenchment, three on Total Dominion, and the rewards climb with them: Fortify 2/4/6, 2/4/6 Arc Cubes, 1/2/3 Xeno Eggs, 1/2/3 Resources.
  • Filling a card pays 1 Progression to whoever holds the most tokens on it. Ties all pay — every player level on the most takes first place and each gains the Progression.
  • Retired with the old deck: Culling the Herd, Blood in the Sand, Grand Expansion and Arms Race, along with second and third place rewards and the tie-drops-a-place rule — there is no second place to drop to any more.

3.12

Rules & wording

  • Deploying into a Suppressed Sector arrives at half maximum Energy rounded down, not up.
  • A neutral Control Point blocks movement exactly as an enemy one does. The manual had listed it as something you could pass through.
  • Tunnel movement costs the tile you arrive on plus 1, with the Hostile Movement Penalty on top — not a flat 2.
  • Contracts run two rounds rather than three, and the duration boxes drop to two.
  • Stockpile becomes an Order: 3 Resources of one type for you, 1 of that type for everyone else. The Contract count drops to 19.
  • A Worker may be assigned to your Contract slot, adding a round to every Contract you play — which buys the third round back.
  • Xeno Lab grants 1 Progression and 1 Xeno Egg when you subjugate a Creature, in place of a Reroll against Creatures.
  • Xenobiologist pays an extra Xeno Egg for eliminating a Creature and Rerolls while defending against one, so it no longer covers the same ground as the Xeno Lab.
  • Subjugating a Creature gives up the Xeno Egg as well as the Progression.
  • The Intermission Phase now branches on where a Creature stands: off the Volcano first, attack if already in Range, otherwise move and check again.
  • The Headquarters lists a Fortify of 10 — you begin with it built, which is where its Control Die's 10 comes from.
  • Advancing a Tier states what it buys: 3, 6 and 9 Arc Cubes for a Tier I, II or III ability.

3.11

Discoveries

  • 18 Discovery Cards added, shuffled through the Ability deck. A Discovery resolves the moment it is drawn, costs nothing and never enters your hand.
  • They are their own card type, not a fourth kind of ability — the manual said the latter, which was never true of the printed cards.
  • Twelve are a windfall and six cost you something: Corrosive Bloom, Fault Line, Unstable Ruins, Corrupted Archive, Tremor and Broken Seal on a roll of 1.
  • Ancient Bulwark Fortifies rather than granting Sector Strength directly.
  • Sealed Crate renamed Broken Seal — a sealed crate does not explain the Creature that comes out of it.
  • Ability Cards, Objectives and Discoveries are all laid out at their printed Poker size, 63.5 × 89 mm. Every deck now shows its true printed face.

3.10

Corporations

  • Storage limits published. 12 Arc Cubes, 3 of each Resource, 4 Xeno Eggs. Anything gained past a limit is lost. Atlas Inc. and Maximum Yield both referred to limits the manual never stated.
  • You begin the game with 1 available Worker, which was never written down either. Axiom grants 2 extra rather than setting a total of 3.
  • A card beats the manual. Where card text contradicts a rule, the card wins — Sentinel Sieges from Range 2, Cinderworks crosses Lava Fields.
  • Frontier moves onto the Fortify keyword and drops from 3 to 2.
  • Helios Global Fortifies instead of raising a Control Die directly, so it now obeys the 6/12 cap and cannot shore up a Suppressed Sector.
  • Core Industries and Citadel Systems name the cost they waive: Resource, or Resource and Worker.
  • Hyperion reads “Arc Cube rewards” and Genesys reads “Movement Detected” — both previously named things that do not exist.
  • Resodyne chooses its Resource type at setup, on a second Start of the game line.
  • A Structure that is replaced rather than destroyed passes its Worker to the replacement.
  • Aurex Systems sets a floor of 1 on its Headquarters Control Die rather than preventing destruction, which left a Control Die sitting at 0 in a state the rules did not describe.
  • Corporation cards are laid out at their printed Mini-Euro size, 44 × 68 mm, in the order of the two print sheets.
  • Objective reward lists shorten the keyword to Fortify X to fit the printed face. The manual notes the shorthand.
  • Three corporations added, each for a system no other card touched: Quartex (Objectives), Tristar (deployment) and Red Sector Inc. (Sectors rendered uninhabitable).

3.9

Suppression & Structures

  • A Suppressed Sector cannot be Fortified, from any source. An enemy standing in your Sector now stops you shoring up its Control Point as well as stopping its income.
  • This closes a stalemate loop where a Foundry could undo an attack every round faster than it could be pressed home.
  • The Foundry drops its own suppression clause, which the general rule now covers.
  • Suppressed is capitalized throughout as the defined state it is.
  • The Medlab is now the Charging Bay, on the Structure and on the Ability Card that builds it. Operatives carry Energy in a powered suit rather than wounds, so a medical name never described what it does.

3.8

Operatives

  • Operative Energy is a single fixed value. It no longer rises with your Tier, so nothing on an Operative Card changes over the course of a game.
  • With that and the Creature change, advancing a Tier now does exactly one thing — it unlocks that Tier's Ability Cards.
  • Roster reworked across two sheets, 36 cards. The old Marksman becomes Reaper, gaining Attack 4 and losing the ability to Siege; a different Marksman arrives on the second sheet.
  • Guardian added: every enemy within Range 3 may only target it, and it Rerolls while defending.
  • The new Marksman is a long-range specialist — 1 extra Bio Damage at Range 4 or more, and it cannot attack anything within Range 2.
  • Saboteur shuts off an adjacent corporation's Structure ability in place of unassigning a Worker.
  • Mercenary is bought for 9 Arc Cubes rather than 12. Bounty Hunter pays 5 and 10 rather than 3 and 8.
  • Six Operatives added, each opening a system the roster did not touch: Interloper (Suppression), Pathfinder (terrain), Foreman (Structures), Seer (the Event Die), Archivist (Ability Cards) and Beastmaster (the subjugated Creature slot).
  • Two more push past the standard stat line, which 21 of the roster shared: Arcwright takes Attack 1 and Defense 1 and is worth no Progression to kill, and Brawler takes Range 1 and cuts a defender's Combat Dice.
  • Engineer Fortifies for 2 Energy instead of 3, and both of its abilities are Active.
  • Demolitionist deals 1 Siege Damage to a Control Point within Range 1 outright, in place of its bonus on a Siege Attack.
  • Deflector triggers on a successful Defense rather than on rolling two Blocks.
  • Scout grants its Movement to all friendly Operatives, with no Range limit.
  • Juggernaut adds 1 to its Defense Roll while defending, in place of blunting Critical Hits.
  • Survivor comes back on 2 Energy rather than 3, and Berserker spends 2 Energy rather than 3.
  • Energy now runs 3–5 across the roster, and Movement 0–4.

3.7

Creatures

  • Creature Health is a single fixed value. The Tier columns are gone from every Encounter Card.
  • Creatures no longer change when a corporation advances a Tier, and that scaling rule is removed from the manual.
  • Health cut across the roster — Sylth Titan and the three Overminds sit at 5, Snaprunner at 2.
  • Sylth Grunt grants a Reroll on Bio Attacks instead of blunting Critical Hits.
  • Spitter no longer rolls at all: it deals a flat 2 Bio Damage.
  • Ravager's low-Health bonus now gives a Reroll while defending.
  • Gorepod's death blast hits Operatives only, no longer other Creatures.
  • Carapoid dies to a fixed 3 Critical Hits.
  • Trigger wording settled on three forms, matching the printed cards: “on a successful Bio Attack”, “on a successful Siege Attack”, and “on a successful Attack” for either kind. “Hit” is no longer a trigger word — it only names a die face.
  • An Attack is successful when it deals 1 or more Damage — now stated in Combat.

3.6

Keywords & Structures

  • Recruit defined: spend 3 Arc Cubes, draw an Operative Card, assign or discard at once.
  • Fortify your Sectors by X defined: add X Sector Strength in total, split across your Control Points as you like.
  • Structures are built onto your Player Board, not the map, and constructing one grants its Fortify value.
  • Structures marked Active or Passive; only an Active ability spends your one use per turn.
  • Structure Resource costs published.
  • Sector Strength capped at 6, or 12 for a Headquarters.
  • Hostile Movement Penalty defined: +1 Movement per tile inside an enemy-controlled Sector.
  • Bio Attacks are limited by the attacker's Range.
  • Frontier, Assembly Line, Undermine, the Engineer and two Objective rewards reworded onto Fortify.

3.5

Combat, economy & the planet

  • Defending simplified on the Combat Die. The Critical face counts as a plain Block while defending, adding 1 to the Defense Roll like either Block face. Attacking is unchanged: each of the 3 Hits adds 1 to the roll, the Critical adds 2, and the 2 Blocks do nothing.
  • Workers may be assigned to Xeno Eggs, granting 1 extra whenever you gain them.
  • A Worker inside a destroyed Structure is removed from the game.
  • A Xeno Egg restores an Operative to full Energy.
  • A Creature that destroys a Control Point renders that Sector permanently uninhabitable.
  • The Intermission Phase runs per Creature: roll the Intermission Die, attack if able, otherwise move and check again.
  • Creature movement direction is read from the Volcano tile, not the Encounter Card.
  • Volcanic Eruption damages only the rolling player's own forces.

3.4

Card stats & Structures

  • Operative and Encounter Card stat blocks cut from 15 values to 4. Movement, Range, Attack and Defense each carried a separate Tier I, II and III value; each is now a single number, printed along the bottom of the card.
  • Bio and Siege damage merged into one Attack value. A card no longer carries separate Bio and Siege numbers — the same dice count is rolled for either kind of attack.
  • Outpost removed. Deploying adjacent to any Control Point you control is now the base rule.
  • Charging Bay added.
  • Starport keeps only its reposition ability.

3.3

Objectives

  • A five-card Objective deck added, with First Contact always on top.
  • Tracker tokens mark contributions, rewards pay by place, and tied players all slide down one place.

3.2

Abilities

  • Duplicate ability names resolved — Second Wind, Sentinel Grid, Silica Heist, Decommissioned and Terminal Decline replace the repeats.
  • Slipstream and Spoil became Counters.
  • Order Seizure and Counter Seizure rewritten to mirror each other.
  • Discovery added as a fourth ability type, resolving free the moment it is drawn.

3.1

Corporations

  • Chronos reworked: a Sector whose Control Point sits at 6 yields an extra Arc Cube.
  • Copy pass across the deck for spelling, capitalization and consistent wording.

3.0

Baseline rules

  • Every Creature restatted. Some now deal Siege Damage, so a Control Point is no longer safe from the wildlife by default.
  • Three Operative slots, the third reserved for a subjugated Creature.
  • Troopers are printed on the Player Board and are not part of the Operative deck.
  • The Initiative Marker never passes — it marks where a round ends.
  • Nothing ever shares a tile; you may pass through anything that is not hostile.
  • A Creature attacks every enemy in Range, Bio Attacks first, in Operative Die order.
  • Anything changed before this point was not kept, so the record starts here.

Creatures

Drawn from the Encounter deck and answerable to nobody. Up to four roam the board at once, moving and attacking during the Intermission Phase — and most of them cannot touch an Outpost or Headquarters at all, which is the first thing to check when one turns up next to your Sector.

Reading a card

Laid out like an Operative Card, with one difference: the boxes count Health rather than Energy, and Creatures have no active abilities — everything they do is passive, resolved for them during the Intermission Phase.

Siege

A Creature only threatens an Outpost or Headquarters if it can perform a Siege Attack. The ones that cannot say so in plain text on the card, and they can still make life miserable by Suppressing the Sector they stand in.
